## Economics to model

Take rate, incentives, refunds, support, density and disintermediation. Split revenue collected, variable service costs, acquisition spend, local fixed costs and working capital. Keep assumptions distinct from actual cohort data.

## Candidate market comparison

Nairobi neighborhood density versus larger fragmented urban markets. These are **buyer-segment hypotheses**, not verified national market rankings. Interview equivalent buyer roles and compare like-for-like collected revenue and retention.

## Proof before expansion

- **Customer outcome:** Successful match contribution, time to match, repeat buyer and seller.
- **Regulatory and execution constraints:** Worker status, consumer protection, payments, permits.
- **Failure mode:** GMV and registered users are not contribution margin or liquidity.

## Research and experiment

1. Choose two countries and one precisely defined buyer segment per market.
2. Obtain actual quotes for price, payment fees, distribution, fulfillment/support and local compliance where applicable.
3. Model conservative, base and optimistic economics with documented input sources.
4. Test a lawful paid pilot; log collected revenue, cohort retention, refunds, variable costs and CAC.
5. Stop when margin is negative without credible improvement, legal feasibility fails or critical user harm remains.
6. Revisit at the next cohort window; never declare a country profitable based only on addressable population.
