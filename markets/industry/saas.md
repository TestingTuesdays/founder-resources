## Economics to model

Annual contract value, seat/usage expansion, procurement, sales cycle, support. Split revenue collected, variable service costs, acquisition spend, local fixed costs and working capital. Keep assumptions distinct from actual cohort data.

## Candidate market comparison

US/UK/Germany enterprise versus Kenya/Nigeria SME segments. These are **buyer-segment hypotheses**, not verified national market rankings. Interview equivalent buyer roles and compare like-for-like collected revenue and retention.

## Proof before expansion

- **Customer outcome:** Paid pilot, qualified pipeline, gross retention, net revenue retention.
- **Regulatory and execution constraints:** SOC2/security review, data localization, enterprise contracts.
- **Failure mode:** A large ACV can be offset by 9-month procurement and costly implementation.

## Research and experiment

1. Choose two countries and one precisely defined buyer segment per market.
2. Obtain actual quotes for price, payment fees, distribution, fulfillment/support and local compliance where applicable.
3. Model conservative, base and optimistic economics with documented input sources.
4. Test a lawful paid pilot; log collected revenue, cohort retention, refunds, variable costs and CAC.
5. Stop when margin is negative without credible improvement, legal feasibility fails or critical user harm remains.
6. Revisit at the next cohort window; never declare a country profitable based only on addressable population.
