## Economics to model

Payer type, clinical oversight, sales cycle, patient support and reimbursement. Split revenue collected, variable service costs, acquisition spend, local fixed costs and working capital. Keep assumptions distinct from actual cohort data.

## Candidate market comparison

US employer/provider buyers versus Kenyan clinic/caregiver workflows. These are **buyer-segment hypotheses**, not verified national market rankings. Interview equivalent buyer roles and compare like-for-like collected revenue and retention.

## Proof before expansion

- **Customer outcome:** Paid provider contracts, safe activation, patient retention and care outcome.
- **Regulatory and execution constraints:** Clinical claims, privacy, professional licensing, procurement.
- **Failure mode:** Clinical value and user engagement do not prove reimbursement or profitability.

## Research and experiment

1. Choose two countries and one precisely defined buyer segment per market.
2. Obtain actual quotes for price, payment fees, distribution, fulfillment/support and local compliance where applicable.
3. Model conservative, base and optimistic economics with documented input sources.
4. Test a lawful paid pilot; log collected revenue, cohort retention, refunds, variable costs and CAC.
5. Stop when margin is negative without credible improvement, legal feasibility fails or critical user harm remains.
6. Revisit at the next cohort window; never declare a country profitable based only on addressable population.
