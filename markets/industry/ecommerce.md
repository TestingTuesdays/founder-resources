# E-commerce and retail economics

[← Sector economics](README.md) · [Profitability framework](../profitability-framework.md) · [Scorecard](../../templates/market-profitability-scorecard.md)

## Economics to model

Product margin, shipping, returns, inventory, duties and payment fees. Split revenue collected, variable service costs, acquisition spend, local fixed costs and working capital. Keep assumptions distinct from actual cohort data.

## Candidate market comparison

Kenya mobile commerce versus Brazil/Indonesia local fulfillment. These are **buyer-segment hypotheses**, not verified national market rankings. Interview equivalent buyer roles and compare like-for-like collected revenue and retention.

## Proof before expansion

- **Customer outcome:** Delivered-order margin, repeat purchase, refund-adjusted CAC.
- **Regulatory and execution constraints:** Consumer returns, import duties, tax and product labeling.
- **Failure mode:** Checkout conversion is not delivered-order profit.

## Research and experiment

1. Choose two countries and one precisely defined buyer segment per market.
2. Obtain actual quotes for price, payment fees, distribution, fulfillment/support and local compliance where applicable.
3. Model conservative, base and optimistic economics with documented input sources.
4. Test a lawful paid pilot; log collected revenue, cohort retention, refunds, variable costs and CAC.
5. Stop when margin is negative without credible improvement, legal feasibility fails or critical user harm remains.
6. Revisit at the next cohort window; never declare a country profitable based only on addressable population.
