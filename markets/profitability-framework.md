# How to evaluate the most profitable market for your startup

[← Market selection](README.md) · [Scorecard](../templates/market-profitability-scorecard.md)

## Define profitability before ranking countries

**Contribution margin per customer** = collected revenue − payment processing − variable hosting/inference − fulfillment − variable support − refunds/fraud losses − other variable costs. For physical goods, include COGS, duties, shipping and returns.

**Customer acquisition payback** = acquisition cost / monthly contribution margin per newly acquired paying customer. This simple approximation assumes stable monthly contribution; cohort churn, annual billing and expansion require a more careful model.

**Expected cohort contribution** = sum of actual or forecast contribution from a cohort over a specified horizon − acquisition spend − allocated market-entry costs. Keep forecasts separate from observed outcomes.

**Market-entry break-even customers** = fixed local entry and ongoing costs / contribution per customer over the matching period, when contribution is positive. Do not treat gross revenue as profit.

## Compare a specific buyer segment in each country

| Criterion | Evidence | What can make it unattractive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Willingness to pay | Interviews, deposits, real paid pilots | High interest but no purchases |
| Reachable demand | Qualified lead counts and channels | Large population but inaccessible buyers |
| Acquisition economics | Tracked spend and activated customers | Expensive paid clicks and low activation |
| Retention | Cohorts with meaningful repeat action | High initial conversion, high churn |
| Gross/contribution margin | Payment, tax, service and fulfillment model | FX, refunds, fraud or inference costs |
| Competition and differentiation | Alternatives, switching costs, procurement | Dominant incumbents and weak differentiation |
| Regulatory feasibility | Current regulator and counsel | Licensing unavailable or expensive |
| Local operations | Staff, banking, support, delivery, language | High fixed setup and long sales cycles |
| Capital and cash cycle | Receivables, inventory, settlement | Cash trapped in long collection cycles |

## Research process

1. Define one product, one buyer and one transaction/value event.
2. Choose 3–5 candidate countries with a clear reason for each.
3. Build a conservative, base and optimistic unit-economics model per country; explicitly label every unverified assumption.
4. Interview comparable buyers and run a limited paid pilot where lawful.
5. Validate merchant onboarding, data, advertising, entity, employment and sector rules before accepting funds or hiring.
6. Measure server-verified activated customers, paid retention, refunds, cash collection and contribution margin.
7. Rank on *risk-adjusted cohort contribution* and feasibility, not just total addressable market.
8. Revisit after an actual cohort and document changes in assumptions.

## Illustrative calculation, not real country data

Market A: $100 collected per customer, $25 variable cost, $50 CAC → $75 contribution before acquisition and $25 after CAC. Market B: $60 collected, $10 variable cost, $15 CAC → $50 before acquisition and $35 after CAC. B can be more profitable per acquired customer despite lower prices. This ignores retention, fixed costs, tax and capital needs.

## Important exclusions

A weighted score is a decision aid, not a validated forecast. High average income, mobile penetration, GDP growth or funding volume cannot establish startup profit. Verify real customer economics and current country-specific laws.
