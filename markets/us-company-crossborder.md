# US-incorporated founder choosing international markets

[← Markets](README.md) · [Kenya filing](../kenya/company-filing.md) · [Cross-border fundraising](../growth/fundraising-us-kenya-structure.md)

## The founder decision tree

**Selling remotely?** Check whether the customer, payment method, contract, tax nexus and regulated activity can be served from the US entity. **Hiring or running local operations?** Review local employment, permanent establishment and subsidiary/branch options. **Holding funds, lending, providing healthcare, energy or transport?** Check activity-specific licensing before assuming software-only exemptions. **Shipping hardware?** Add import classification, safety conformity, warranty, spare parts and distributor obligations.

## Compare structures

- US-only seller: simplest corporate structure, but local taxes, consumer law, data and licensing may still apply.
- US parent with local subsidiary: useful for hiring and contracting, but creates intercompany accounting and governance.
- Branch: may be appropriate for direct foreign-company operations, with distinct registration and liability questions.
- Reseller/distributor: can reduce initial fixed footprint but requires margin sharing, quality controls and contract safeguards.

## Unit-economics decision

Build a country-specific model of price, actual collected revenue, payment/FX fees, tax treatment, service/hosting, local support, refunds, acquisition cost, retention and local fixed cost. Do not treat investor interest or addressable population as profit.

## Qualified advice

Coordinate US CPA/securities counsel and in-market tax/legal/company specialists. Confirm current obligations for the exact legal entity and activity; do not assume a universal US–Kenya or US–international tax rule.
