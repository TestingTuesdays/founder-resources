# How to establish and operate a business in a new country

## Choose operating model before incorporation

| Model | Useful for | Risks and questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-border sales from existing company | Early remote pilots | Tax nexus, VAT/GST, data, payment acceptance, sector restrictions |
| Local distributor or reseller | Physical products and relationship sales | Channel economics, warranties, anti-bribery, contract enforcement |
| Employer of record or local contractors | Limited local staffing | Employment classification, IP, permanent establishment, data handling |
| Local subsidiary | Sustained hiring, contracts, operations | Incorporation, payroll, corporate tax, intercompany agreements |
| Registered foreign-company branch | Direct local operations | Local registration, liability, tax and representative requirements |
| Joint venture | Regulated/relationship-heavy markets | Control, governance, conflicts and ownership restrictions |

## Launch sequence

1. Define actual commercial activity, buyers, contract party, payments, goods/data flows and where staff work.
2. Validate demand through lawful low-cost interviews and paid pilots before establishing expensive fixed infrastructure.
3. Ask local legal/tax advisors whether registration, local entity, permanent establishment, VAT, employment or sector licensing applies.
4. Confirm bank account, payment processor eligibility, FX settlement, invoicing, refunds and tax documentation.
5. Review data protection, cross-border transfer, cybersecurity, accessibility and advertising rules.
6. If physical goods: calculate landed cost including customs classification, import duties, freight, storage, warranty and returns; verify conformity requirements.
7. If hiring: evaluate payroll, social contributions, immigration/work permits, employment agreements and contractor status.
8. Prepare local-language terms, support, cancellation, dispute and incident escalation.
9. Track cash collections, gross/contribution margin, CAC, retention, working capital and compliance costs.
10. Choose scale, partner, pivot or exit based on actual economics and legal feasibility.

## US company operating abroad

Delaware incorporation is not a substitute for local compliance. A US parent can own a local subsidiary, but ownership, IP, transfer pricing, intercompany loans, employment and tax reporting need review. US securities and foreign reporting obligations may also apply.

- [India MCA](https://www.mca.gov.in/), [Invest India](https://www.investindia.gov.in/)

- [Brazil Receita Federal](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br), [Invest & Export Brasil](https://www.gov.br/empresas-e-negocios/)

Links are research entry points, not confirmation of a current permit, tax rate or deadline. Obtain current local advice before filing.
